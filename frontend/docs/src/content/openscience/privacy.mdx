OpenScience works with local projects, connected model providers, and optional online tools. Which data leaves your device depends on the connections and actions you use, as well as your trace-sharing settings.

## Know where data goes

| Surface | Data involved |
| --- | --- |
| Local project and app storage | Working files, saved conversations, scratch data, Results, and settings. |
| Selected model route | The conversation context and attachments sent for inference. Ace uses a managed route; your own connection uses its provider. |
| Search, connectors, and remote compute | Queries, selected inputs, and files required by the specific service or task. |
| Session trace sharing | Diagnostic conversation and tool records uploaded under the account and device privacy settings. |

Choosing a local model removes the remote model request. It does not disable online tools or signed-in trace sharing. Review both before assuming a task is offline.

## Review session trace sharing

While signed in, session traces are shared by default, including sessions using your own keys, subscriptions, and local models. Existing opt-outs remain in effect. Traces can include prompts and model context, provider-visible reasoning and responses, tool inputs and outputs, searches, errors, and reported usage.

Open **Customize → Permissions → Data & privacy → Share session traces** to turn off uploads from this device and discard queued and rejected records. Account preferences can disable sharing across devices or exclude user-owned routes. The device setting does not override an account opt-out. Signing out also stops uploads.

The **Logging health** row shows queued, acknowledged and rejected-record counts from this device. Delivery errors appear separately so the counts stay visible. **Last acknowledgement** shows the most recent confirmed delivery time; rejected records remain local and are not retried automatically. **Refresh** reads current status without forcing an upload. Acknowledgements alone do not verify complete usage or charges. **Check server** separately requests a read-only report for the signed-in account, this device and the selected workspace over the last 24 hours. It checks workspace-scoped delivery receipts (including deliveries without stored events), response-to-usage projection, and managed call/hold/ledger agreement. Pending settlement and unknown provider fields are distinct from confirmed discrepancies. The report cannot see queued or rejected device records, or requests rejected before a receipt. New replay-only deliveries are reported separately from missing accepted events; historical receipts without workspace attribution can only be checked when associated with stored events. No stored responses is shown as **No data**, and an older or unreachable server is explicitly unavailable. Checking does not upload traces, change consent, debit credits or repair records.

Local call-attempt metadata and trace-sharing delivery records are saved in the shared `research.sqlite` database. A model HTTP attempt is recorded before dispatch, including retries and requests that fail or are cancelled before a response. Calls without provider usage remain unknown. Diagnostic metadata stays local when sharing is off; disabling sharing discards upload payloads and trace bindings, including pending and rejected payloads in the migrated queue backup.

Logging health also shows the oldest queued record, flags waits longer than five minutes, and lists the latest delivery attempt and rejected event using opaque IDs and HTTP status codes. It does not display server response bodies. A pending attempt means no completed acknowledgement is stored; it does not prove that the server received nothing.

Temporary delivery failures can leave queued records waiting for automatic retry. Large text may be truncated and binary attachments represented by metadata. Known credentials are redacted, but private research can still appear in prompts and tool output.

For support investigations, `openscience debug audit <session>` prints a local, content-free report for that conversation, its internal title and summary calls, and its worker sessions. It includes local and opaque telemetry session IDs, routes, models, timestamps, call IDs, provider request IDs, recorded token counts when a visible step has them, and any managed hold IDs, but not prompts, responses, tool payloads, or session titles. The command does not upload anything or bypass trace-sharing preferences.

The audit’s `cost` field labels ChatGPT and other subscription routes as `subscription` with a null amount, rather than implying that subscription access is free. New calls retain `reportedCostUSD` separately when the provider supplies it; a reported zero remains zero. `recordedCostUSD` preserves historical step accounting and may be a calculation rather than a receipt. New completed steps label catalog estimates as `catalog_estimate` and calculated Ace amounts as `wallet_calculated`; these are not settled receipts. Older saved steps without cost provenance remain `recorded_unclassified`; calls without cost evidence are `unknown`. Provider-reported amounts are not proof of a settled Ace charge, and subscription fees are not allocated per call.

The [privacy policy](https://openscience.sh/privacy) describes retention and account deletion controls. Disabling future uploads and deleting already stored account data are different actions. Review the scope of the deletion control before confirming it; telemetry deletion does not remove your local project, revoke external credentials, or reverse billing records.

## Control file and tool access

Connect only the folders needed for the project. Read approval requests before granting broader filesystem access, running code, publishing, or sending data to a service. See [Permissions](/openscience/permissions), [Projects](/openscience/projects), and [Connectors](/openscience/connectors).

Instructions can guide how the agent handles data, but they are not a substitute for folder permissions or service access controls. Retrieved papers, pages, and tool output are evidence to inspect, not authority to disclose unrelated files or secrets.

## Handle keys and exports

Keep secrets in connection settings, supported environment variables, or your secret manager. Do not place them in prompts, project instructions, example files, or shared repositories. Removing a local connection does not revoke the key at its provider.

Conversation exports and saved Results can contain research material. Inspect them before sharing and include only data the recipient may access. Account sign-in and trace uploads are not full project backups; keep your own copies of inputs, code, and outputs. See [Files and storage](/openscience/files) and [Share and hand off research](/openscience/team-workflows).

## Report a privacy or security issue

Use private account support for account data and payment details. Follow the [security reporting policy](https://github.com/synthetic-sciences/openscience/blob/main/SECURITY.md) for vulnerabilities. A public issue should contain a minimal reproduction with credentials and private research removed.


## Inspect per-call usage

Open a conversation’s context indicator to see **Model call usage**. Each completed step shows its access route, model, provider token counts, cost basis, call ID and any recorded Ace hold ID. Provider-reported input, output, reasoning, cache-read and cache-write counts remain separate; an omitted field or an older record without that evidence shows **Unknown**, while a reported zero stays zero. Counts are not added together because some providers include reasoning in output and cache reads in input.

Provider-reported amounts, calculated Ace amounts and catalog estimates have separate subtotals for this session. Child agents have their own sessions. Subscription fees are not allocated per call. The historical accounting total may include estimates; use the per-call basis before interpreting a value as a charge.
